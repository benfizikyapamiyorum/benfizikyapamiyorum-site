Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 · 4.3.2 Series and parallel circuits

Series and Parallel Circuits

Try this: Switch between Series and Parallel and change the resistors. In series the current is the same everywhere and the p.d. is shared. In parallel each resistor gets the full p.d. and the currents add up.

Moving dots show the direction of conventional current (from + to −). Faster dots = larger current. The cell and meters are ideal.

Check yourself

Two 6 Ω resistors are connected in parallel. What is their combined resistance?

Show answer
1/R = 1/6 + 1/6 = 2/6, so R = 3 Ω. The combined resistance of resistors in parallel is less than either resistor on its own.
