The force that keeps a planet in orbit is the gravitational attraction of the Sun. The Sun's field gets weaker further away, so outer planets move more slowly and take longer to go round.

The seasons

Time

Earth spins once in about 24 hours, the Moon orbits in about a month (29.5 days from new Moon to new Moon) and the Earth orbits the Sun in about 365 days. Moon phases here are not matched to real dates.

Readings

How far does light travel? Core

1 light-year = distance light travels in one year in space
= 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s × (365 × 24 × 3600 s) ≈ 9.5 × 10¹⁵ m

The Sun as a star Core

The Sun is a medium-sized star, made mostly of hydrogen and helium. It radiates most of its energy as infrared, visible light and ultraviolet.
Stars are powered by nuclear fusion: in a stable star hydrogen nuclei fuse to make helium, releasing energy.
The Sun is one of many billions of stars in our galaxy, the Milky Way (about 100 000 light-years across). The Universe contains many billions of galaxies.

Supplement Light from distant galaxies is redshifted: the further away a galaxy is, the faster it is moving away. This, and the cosmic microwave background radiation (CMBR), supports the Big Bang theory. Hubble constant H₀ = v ÷ d ≈ 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁸ per second.
