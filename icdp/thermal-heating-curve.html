Core On the flat sections the heater is still supplying energy, but the temperature does not change: the energy is changing the state (melting or boiling), not raising the temperature. On the sloping sections the internal energy increases because the average kinetic energy of the particles increases.

Check yourself

1. Core Why does the temperature stay at 0 °C while the ice is melting?

Energy is still being supplied. It is used to separate the particles from their fixed positions (changing solid to liquid), so the average kinetic energy of the particles, and so the temperature, does not change.

2. Core At standard atmospheric pressure, what are the melting point and boiling point of pure water?

Pure water melts at 0 °C and boils at 100 °C at standard atmospheric pressure.

3. Supplement How much energy is needed to heat 0.50 kg of water from 20 °C to 80 °C? (c = 4200 J/(kg °C))

ΔE = m c ΔT = 0.50 × 4200 × 60 = 126 000 J.

4. Supplement A 2000 W heater is switched on for 63 s in 1.0 kg of water. Assuming no energy is lost, what is the temperature rise? (c = 4200 J/(kg °C))

E = P t = 2000 × 63 = 126 000 J. ΔT = E ÷ (m c) = 126 000 ÷ (1.0 × 4200) = 30 °C.

5. Core What happens to the particles while the liquid water is warming from 0 °C to 100 °C?

A rise in temperature means an increase in the average kinetic energy of the particles: they move faster.
