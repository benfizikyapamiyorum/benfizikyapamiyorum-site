Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 · 1.5.1 Effects of forces

Stretching a Spring

Try this: Add 100 g masses one at a time and read the length on the ruler. Each reading is plotted on the load–extension graph. Keep going until the graph stops being a straight line: that is the limit of proportionality.

The experiment

Load–extension graph

Controls

Natural (unstretched) length of the spring = 10.0 cm. Load = weight of the masses = mg, with g = 9.8 N/kg.

extension = stretched length − natural length
Below the limit of proportionality: F = kx (load is proportional to extension).

Check yourself

A spring extends by 3.0 cm when a load of 6.0 N hangs from it (below its limit of proportionality). What is its spring constant?

Show answer
k = F ÷ x = 6.0 N ÷ 0.030 m = 200 N/m. Remember to change the extension into metres first.
