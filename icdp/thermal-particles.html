T (in K) = θ (in °C) + 273. At absolute zero (−273 °C) the particles have the least possible kinetic energy. Thermal expansion: as the temperature rises the particles move more and take up more space (exaggerated here). Gases expand most, solids least.

2. Evaporation

Particles have a range of energies. Only the most energetic particles near the surface can escape. The thermometer shows the trend only (not to scale).

Evaporation is faster with a higher temperature, a larger surface area and air moving over the surface. Escaping particles take more than their share of kinetic energy, so the average kinetic energy of the liquid left behind falls: it cools.

3. Transfer of thermal energy

Check yourself

1. Core Which statement describes the particles in a liquid?

In a liquid the particles are close together in an irregular arrangement and can slide past each other. Solids: fixed, regular, vibrating. Gases: far apart, fast and random.

2. Core Why does evaporation cool the liquid left behind?

Only the more energetic particles near the surface escape. The particles left behind have a lower average kinetic energy, so the liquid's temperature is lower.

3. Core What is 27 °C on the kelvin scale?

T = θ + 273 = 27 + 273 = 300 K.

4. Core Why does hot water rise above the heater in a convection current?

The heated water expands, so its density decreases and it rises. Cooler, denser water sinks to take its place, making a convection current.

5. Core Two identical cans, one dull black and one shiny silver, are placed the same distance from a heater. Which warms up faster?

Dull black surfaces are good absorbers (and good emitters) of infrared radiation. Shiny surfaces reflect most of it.
