Check yourself

1. Core A gas is squashed into a smaller volume at constant temperature. Why does its pressure increase?

The same particles at the same average speed have less space, so they hit each part of the wall more often. More collisions per second on each unit area means a greater pressure.

2. Supplement 120 cm³ of gas at 100 kPa is compressed slowly to 40 cm³ at constant temperature. What is its new pressure?

p₂ = p₁V₁ ÷ V₂ = 100 × 120 ÷ 40 = 300 kPa. One-third of the volume gives three times the pressure.

3. Supplement Gas at 200 kPa occupies 50 cm³. It expands to 125 cm³ at constant temperature. What is its new pressure?

p₂ = 200 × 50 ÷ 125 = 80 kPa.

4. Supplement What does a graph of p against 1/V look like for a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature?

p = constant × (1/V), so p is proportional to 1/V: a straight line through the origin. The p against V graph is a curve.
