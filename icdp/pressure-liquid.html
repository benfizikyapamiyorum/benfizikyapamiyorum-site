Pressure in Liquids

Try this: Drag gauge A or B up and down in the tank (or use the sliders), then change the liquid. The jets show how hard the liquid is pushed out of each hole. Below, stand a brick on different faces to see how pressure depends on area.
Predict first: which hole will the liquid come out of fastest: the top, middle or bottom one? If you swap the water for a denser liquid, will the pressure at the same depth go up, go down or stay the same?

The tank is kept topped up, so the surface stays at the same level. Gauges show the pressure due to the liquid only; the atmosphere adds about 100 kPa on top.

Check yourself

1. Core Where is the pressure greatest in a tank of water?

Pressure in a liquid increases with depth, because there is more liquid above pushing down. The bottom hole has the fastest jet.

2. Supplement What is the pressure due to the water at a depth of 5.0 m? (density of water = 1000 kg/m³)

Δp = ρ g Δh = 1000 × 9.8 × 5.0 = 49 000 Pa (49 kPa).

3. Supplement In oil of density 800 kg/m³, what is the pressure difference between depths of 0.20 m and 0.70 m?

Δh = 0.70 − 0.20 = 0.50 m, so Δp = 800 × 9.8 × 0.50 = 3920 Pa.

4. Core A person of weight 600 N stands on a total shoe area of 0.040 m². What pressure do they exert on the floor?

p = F ÷ A = 600 N ÷ 0.040 m² = 15 000 Pa.

5. Core Why is a dam built much thicker at the bottom than at the top?

The pressure of the water increases with depth, so the bottom of the dam must withstand the largest pressure.
